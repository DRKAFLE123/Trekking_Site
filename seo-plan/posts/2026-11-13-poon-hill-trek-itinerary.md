# Poon Hill Trek Itinerary: 3, 4 & 5 Days, Distance & Difficulty

**Quick answer:** The Poon Hill trek takes 3 to 5 days from Pokhara. The classic loop goes up through Ulleri to Ghorepani, climbs Poon Hill (3,210m) for sunrise, and comes down through Tadapani and Ghandruk. Walked in full from Nayapul it is about 49km; with a jeep at both ends it is closer to 25km. It is an easy to moderate trek, and its one hard part is the long stone staircase to Ulleri.

## The three itineraries at a glance

| | 3 days | 4 days | 5 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Start | Jeep to Ulleri or Tikhedhunga | Jeep to Tikhedhunga | Drive to Nayapul |
| Nights | Ghorepani, Tadapani | Ulleri or Banthanti, Ghorepani, Tadapani | Tikhedhunga, Ghorepani, Tadapani, Ghandruk |
| End | Jeep from Ghandruk | Jeep from Ghandruk | Walk to Nayapul, or jeep from Ghandruk |
| Walking | about 24 to 27km | about 26 to 28km | about 36 to 49km |
| Longest day | 6 to 7 hours | 6 to 7 hours | 6 to 7 hours |
| Best for | Fit trekkers short on time | Most people | Beginners, families, older trekkers |

Distances are approximate; published figures for the same sections vary by a kilometre or two. Climb and terrain matter more than kilometres here: one hour of stone steps is harder than three hours on a forest path.

## 5-day Poon Hill itinerary (the full walk)

This is the walking part of our [Poon Hill trek](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/poon-hill-trek) and the gentlest pace.

| Day | Route | Distance | Time | Height change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Drive Pokhara to Nayapul (1,070m), walk to Tikhedhunga (1,540m) | about 9km | 3 to 4 hours | about +470m |
| 2 | Tikhedhunga to Ghorepani (2,850m) via Ulleri | about 9.5km | 5 to 7 hours | about +1,310m |
| 3 | Sunrise on Poon Hill (3,210m), then Ghorepani to Tadapani (2,630m) | about 11km | 6 to 7 hours in total | about +850m and −970m |
| 4 | Tadapani to Ghandruk (1,940m) | about 6km | 2.5 to 4 hours | about −690m |
| 5 | Ghandruk to Nayapul, drive to Pokhara | about 13km | 4 to 5 hours | about −870m |

On day 5 you can shorten the walk by taking a jeep from Ghandruk or nearby Kimche, which cuts the total walking from about 49km to about 36km.

## 4-day Poon Hill itinerary

| Day | Route | Distance | Time | Height change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jeep Pokhara to Tikhedhunga (2 to 3 hours), walk the steps to Ulleri (2,080m) or Banthanti (about 2,210m) | about 3 to 4km | 2.5 to 3.5 hours | about +550 to +670m |
| 2 | Ulleri or Banthanti to Ghorepani (2,850m) | about 6 to 7km | 3 to 4 hours | about +640 to +770m |
| 3 | Sunrise on Poon Hill, then Ghorepani to Tadapani (2,630m) | about 11km | 6 to 7 hours | about +850m and −970m |
| 4 | Tadapani to Ghandruk (1,940m), jeep to Pokhara (2 to 3 hours) | about 6km | 2.5 to 4 hours | about −690m |

Splitting the climb over two short days is the easiest way to deal with the Ulleri steps, and it means you reach Ghorepani fresh for the sunrise.

## 3-day Poon Hill itinerary

| Day | Route | Distance | Time | Height change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Jeep Pokhara to Ulleri (or Tikhedhunga), walk to Ghorepani (2,850m) | about 7 to 10km | 4 to 7 hours | about +770 to +1,310m |
| 2 | Sunrise on Poon Hill, then Ghorepani to Tadapani (2,630m) | about 11km | 6 to 7 hours | about +850m and −970m |
| 3 | Tadapani to Ghandruk (1,940m), jeep to Pokhara | about 6km | 2.5 to 4 hours | about −690m |

Jeeps now reach Ulleri, and sometimes Banthanti, on a rough road, which skips most of the steps. How far they go depends on the season and the state of the road, so plan for Tikhedhunga and treat Ulleri as a bonus. Some 3-day plans go straight from Ghorepani to Ghandruk on day 2; that is a 9 to 10 hour day and only suits strong walkers.

## The sunrise hike to Poon Hill

You leave Ghorepani at about 4:30 to 5am with a headlamp. The climb is about 1.5km each way and takes 45 minutes to an hour up. It is steep but short. From the top you see Dhaulagiri, Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre as the sun comes up. A small entrance fee, reported as about NPR 100, is collected below the viewpoint, and there is usually a tea stall at the top. Expect company; in season the viewpoint is busy.

## The Ulleri steps: the hardest part

Between Tikhedhunga (1,540m) and Ulleri (2,080m) the trail climbs more than 3,000 stone steps. This is the part people remember. Allow about two hours, take it slowly, and start early so you climb in the shade.

The steps are hard on tired legs but safe: wide, well made and used every day by villagers and mules. Give way to mule trains on the inside of the trail, away from the drop. Poles help a lot, going up and even more coming down.

## How difficult is the Poon Hill trek?

Easy to moderate by Himalayan standards. There is no technical ground, there is a lodge or tea stop every hour or so, and you sleep below 3,000m every night, so altitude sickness is rarely a problem. A mild headache at Ghorepani is possible; walk slowly, drink plenty and tell your guide if you feel unwell. Our guide to [preventing altitude sickness](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/how-to-prevent-altitude-sickness-while-trekking-in-nepal) lists the warning signs.

You do not need trekking experience, but you do need basic fitness. If you don't already walk regularly, four to six weeks of preparation is plenty:

- Two or three walks a week, building up to 3 or 4 hours on hills
- Stairs, with a light backpack, to prepare for Ulleri
- Practice walks in the boots you plan to wear

Beginners most often struggle with sore knees on the way down and with new boots, not with fitness. Poles and broken-in boots solve most of it.

## Poon Hill with children or older trekkers

Many families do Poon Hill. Trekking companies run family versions for children from about age 6, though there is no official minimum. Choose the 5-day plan, or the 4-day plan with a night in Ulleri, hire a porter, and bring warm layers for the dawn hike. Young children may not be able to describe altitude symptoms, so treat unusual tiredness or loss of appetite seriously.

For older trekkers, age matters less than knees. Take the slower plan, use poles, and allow extra time on the long descent from Tadapani to Ghandruk.

## Permits and fees

You need the Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP), NPR 3,000 for foreign trekkers. Agencies also arrange a TIMS card. See our [trekking permits page](https://natureheaventreks.com/travel-info/trekking-permits-and-fees) for current fees, plus the small Poon Hill entrance fee on the morning of the sunrise.

## Do you need a guide for Poon Hill?

Nepal announced in 2023 that foreign trekkers need a licensed guide on many trekking routes, and agency lists of those routes include Poon Hill-ABC. In practice, reports from the Annapurna region are inconsistent: some describe checks at checkpoints, others say guides are not asked for. We could not find a current official notice that settles it, so ask when you book, and check again before you travel.

Rules aside, a guide is worth having on a first trek. The route is not hard to follow, but a guide sets the pace, handles lodges and jeeps, watches for altitude symptoms and knows what to do if something goes wrong. A porter makes the steps far easier.

## Best time for Poon Hill, including winter

Poon Hill can be trekked for most of the year because of its low altitude.

- **March to April:** rhododendron forests in bloom, the best-known season here. See our [rhododendron forest guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/rhododendron-forest-nepal).
- **October to November:** the clearest skies and the busiest lodges.
- **December to February:** quiet and clear. Daytime in Ghorepani is about 5 to 10°C, but the dawn hike is below freezing and can feel like −8 to −10°C with wind. Stone steps can be icy early in the morning, so start later on stair days. See our [best treks in Nepal in December](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/best-treks-in-nepal-in-december).
- **June to September:** monsoon. Rain, leeches and clouds over the peaks, and jeeps may not get past the lower road. Not recommended for a first trek.

## What to pack

You do not need specialist kit outside winter. The essentials: broken-in boots, poles, a warm jacket for the dawn hike, a headlamp, layers, a rain shell, and a sleeping bag or liner in the colder months. Our [packing list](https://natureheaventreks.com/packing-list) has the full version.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many days do you need for Poon Hill?

Three to five days from Pokhara. Four days is the most comfortable for most people. Add a day each way to get between Kathmandu and Pokhara.

### How long is the Poon Hill trek in km?

About 25km with jeeps at both ends, and about 49km if you walk the full loop from Nayapul and back.

### Can you do Poon Hill in 3 days?

Yes, if you take a jeep to Ulleri or Tikhedhunga and back from Ghandruk. The first day is a long climb, so it suits people who already hike.

### Where does the Poon Hill trek start?

At Nayapul for the full walk, or higher up at Tikhedhunga or Ulleri by jeep. All are reached from Pokhara.

### How many steps are there on the Poon Hill trek?

More than 3,000 stone steps between Tikhedhunga and Ulleri. Numbers quoted vary, usually around 3,200 to 3,300. Starting by jeep at Ulleri skips most of them.

### How high is Poon Hill?

3,210m (10,530ft). Most trekkers sleep at Ghorepani, about 350m lower, and hike up for sunrise.

### Is the Poon Hill trek easy?

Easy to moderate. There is no technical ground and altitude is low, but the climb to Ulleri and Ghorepani is a tiring day.

### Can a 6-year-old do the Poon Hill trek?

Some do, with the 5-day plan and a porter. It depends on whether your child already walks for several hours at a time.

### Can I trek Poon Hill without a guide?

The trail itself is easy to follow, but the rules are unclear. Nepal's 2023 guide requirement is listed for this route by agencies, and enforcement is reported as inconsistent. Check when you book.
