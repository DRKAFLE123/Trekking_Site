# Can You Trek in Nepal Without a Guide? 2026 Rules by Region

**Quick answer:** Officially, mostly no. Since 1 April 2023, Nepal has required foreign trekkers in its national parks and conservation areas to trek with a licensed guide (or porter) arranged through a registered agency. In practice, the Everest region's local government has never applied the rule, the Annapurna region is reported as inconsistent, and restricted areas such as Manaslu and Upper Mustang are strict. If you want to trek without a guide in 2026, Everest is the realistic option; everywhere else, plan on a guide.

## What the rule says

On 31 March 2023, the Nepal Tourism Board announced that from the next day foreign trekkers could not enter national parks and conservation areas "without a guide or a porter for the entire journey", and that every foreign trekker needs a TIMS card issued through a registered agency. The [Kathmandu Post](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2023/03/31/nepal-tourism-board-has-issued-new-guidelines-for-trekkers-here-is-what-you-need-to-know) listed 44 covered trails across Kanchenjunga, Makalu Barun, Everest, Rolwaling, Langtang, Helambu, Manaslu, Annapurna, Mustang, Dolpa and others.

The rule does not cover day hikes around Kathmandu and Pokhara, and it was never about group size: one trekker with one licensed guide meets it.

### Everest: the exception

When the national rule came in, the Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality, which runs the Everest region, [refused to apply it](https://kathmandupost.com/money/2023/04/03/everest-region-nepal-tourism-board-dispute-over-solo-trek-ban-heats-up). It said a guide is not mandatory and introduced its own trek card instead of the TIMS card. The Tourism Board called that stance irresponsible, but independent trekkers have continued to walk to [Everest Base Camp](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/everest-base-camp-trek) since. On paper, the national list still includes Everest routes, so this could change; check before you travel.

On an independent Everest trek you still pay the local trek card and the Sagarmatha National Park entry fee, and you fly to Lukla or walk in from Jiri or Salleri.

### Annapurna: unclear

The Annapurna region, including the [Poon Hill](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/poon-hill-trek-for-beginners) and [Annapurna Base Camp](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/annapurna-base-camp-trek) treks, is on the 2023 list. Reports from the trail are mixed: some describe guide checks at the conservation area checkpoints, others say guides are not asked for. We could not find a current official notice that settles it. If you plan to go without a guide, ask when you book and check again just before you travel, because enforcement can change from one season to the next.

### Restricted areas: a guide is required

Restricted areas need a special permit from the Department of Immigration, and it is only issued through a registered agency to trekkers with a licensed guide. That covers the [Manaslu Circuit](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/manaslu-circuit-trek), the Tsum Valley, Upper Mustang, Nar Phu, Dolpo and Kanchenjunga, among others. Some agencies report that since March 2026 a single trekker with a guide can get the permit without a second trekker; the guide is still required either way. See our [Manaslu permit costs](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/manaslu-circuit-trek-cost-breakdown) and the [Manaslu guide question](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/does-the-manaslu-circuit-trek-need-a-guide).

## What happens if you trek without a guide where one is required?

When the rule was announced, the Kathmandu Post reported a fine of NPR 12,000 for a trekker found on a designated trail without a guide or TIMS card. In practice, the more common outcome reported by agencies is being stopped at a checkpoint and told to come back with a guide, which can cost you days.

## What a guide costs in 2026

A licensed trekking guide usually costs about **US$25–35 a day**, which normally includes the guide's food, lodging and insurance. A porter is about US$18–25 a day and carries up to 20–25kg, often shared between two trekkers. On a 12-day trek, a guide adds roughly US$300–420 to your budget, and a porter roughly US$220–300.

Booking through an agency also covers permits and the TIMS card, transport and lodge bookings; see our [trekking permits page](https://natureheaventreks.com/travel-info/trekking-permits-and-fees).

## Is a guide worth it anyway?

Rules aside, most first-time trekkers in Nepal get more from the trip with a guide:

- **Altitude:** a guide watches for altitude sickness, sets a sensible pace and knows when to turn around. Read our guide to [preventing altitude sickness](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/how-to-prevent-altitude-sickness-while-trekking-in-nepal).
- **Logistics:** lodges in peak season, jeeps, flights and permits are handled for you.
- **Emergencies:** a guide can organise a helicopter evacuation and deal with your insurer. Check your [travel insurance](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/travel-insurance-for-trekking-in-nepal) covers it.
- **Off-season safety:** in winter, trails can be empty for hours, and trekkers have gone missing walking alone.
- **Culture:** a local guide explains villages, monasteries and food you would otherwise walk past.

## If you want to trek independently

- Choose the Everest region, where independent trekking is still possible, and stick to the main trail.
- Walk with other trekkers rather than alone, and tell your lodge each morning where you are heading.
- Carry insurance that covers helicopter evacuation to the highest point of your trek.
- Get a local SIM with data; coverage is patchy but improving. See our guide to [internet and phone signal on Nepal treks](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/internet-phone-signal-nepal-treks).
- Check the current rules before you fly, not only before you book.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is solo trekking banned in Nepal?

Trekking alone without a guide has been against the national rule in parks and conservation areas since April 2023, but you can trek as a single traveller with your own guide. The Everest region's local government does not require a guide.

### Can I trek to Everest Base Camp without a guide in 2026?

Yes, in practice. The Khumbu Pasang Lhamu Rural Municipality does not require a guide and issues its own trek card. You still pay the Sagarmatha National Park fee.

### Can I trek the Annapurna Circuit or ABC without a guide?

The Annapurna region is on the 2023 list, but the rule is reported as inconsistently applied. Ask when you book and check again before you go.

### Do I need a guide for Langtang?

Langtang is on the 2023 list and agencies report checks at the park entry, so plan on a guide.

### Do I need a guide for the Manaslu Circuit?

Yes. Manaslu is a restricted area, and the permit is only issued through a registered agency for trekkers with a licensed guide.

### How much does a trekking guide cost in Nepal?

About US$25–35 a day for a licensed guide and US$18–25 a day for a porter, usually including their food and lodging.

### Do I still need a TIMS card?

On routes covered by the 2023 rule, yes, issued through a registered agency: NPR 2,000 for most foreigners and NPR 1,000 for SAARC nationals, according to the 2023 notice. Restricted-area permit holders and the Everest region (which uses its own trek card) are handled differently.
