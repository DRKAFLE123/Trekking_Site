# Best Treks in Nepal for Beginners: 7 Easy Routes Ranked

**Quick answer:** The best trek in Nepal for beginners is **Poon Hill**: four or five days on the trail, lodges every night and a highest point of only 3,210m. If you want to see Everest, the **Everest View trek** reaches 3,880m in a week. With a bit more fitness, **Mardi Himal**, **Langtang Valley** and a short **Annapurna Base Camp** trek are all realistic first treks if you take them slowly.

## Beginner treks ranked, easiest first

| Rank | Trek | Our trip | Highest point | Highest night | What makes it harder |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Poon Hill | 7 days | 3,210m | about 2,850m | 3,000+ stone steps to Ulleri |
| 2 | Khopra Danda | 7 days | about 3,660m | about 3,660m | Quiet, simpler lodges higher up |
| 3 | Everest View | 7 days | 3,880m | 3,440m (Namche) | Lukla flight delays; steep climb to Namche |

| 7 | Annapurna Base Camp | 13 days | 4,130m | 4,130m | Length; more days at altitude |

"Our trip" includes the days in Kathmandu and Pokhara. Walking days are usually 4 to 6 hours, and every route has lodges with food each night.

## 1. Poon Hill: the easiest classic trek

[Poon Hill](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/poon-hill-trek) is the trek most first-timers choose, and for good reason. You walk through rhododendron forest and Gurung villages, sleep below 3,000m every night, and climb Poon Hill (3,210m) at dawn for a view of Dhaulagiri and Annapurna South. The only hard part is the long stone staircase to Ulleri. Our [Poon Hill itinerary guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/poon-hill-trek-for-beginners) has 3, 4 and 5-day plans with distances.

## 2. Khopra Danda: Poon Hill without the crowds

The [Khopra Danda trek](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/khopra-dada-trek) starts on the same trails as Poon Hill, then climbs to a ridge at about 3,660m facing Dhaulagiri and Annapurna South. It is a little higher and much quieter, with community-run lodges. A good choice if you are fit and want space.

## 3. Everest View: see Everest in a week

You fly to Lukla, walk to Namche Bazaar and climb to the Everest View Hotel (3,880m) for a view of Everest, Lhotse and Ama Dablam. The [Everest View trek](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/everest-view-trek) takes seven days and you sleep no higher than Namche (3,440m). The climb to Namche is steep, and Lukla flights are often delayed, so keep a spare day.

## 4. Langtang Valley: no flight needed

The [Langtang Valley trek](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/langtang-valley-trek) starts with a drive from Kathmandu and follows a river valley up to Kyanjin Gompa (3,870m), among yak pastures and glaciers. Climbing a viewpoint such as Kyanjin Ri (4,773m) is optional. Since the August 2026 floods, treks start from Dhunche or Thulo Syabru instead of Syabrubesi; TAAN declared the valley open again in late October. Our [Langtang beginner's guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/langtang-valley-trek-a-complete-beginner-s-guide) covers the route.

## 5. Mardi Himal: big mountain views on a short trek

[Mardi Himal](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/mardi-himal-trek) climbs a ridge straight towards Machhapuchhre and reaches 4,500m at Mardi Himal Base Camp. Most days are short, but the day from High Camp to Base Camp and back is long and steep. It is a good second trek, or a first trek for fit hikers. Read our [Mardi Himal difficulty guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/mardi-himal-trek-difficulty) and [Mardi Himal cost guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/mardi-himal-trek-cost).

## 6 and 7. Annapurna Base Camp: the biggest beginner trek

Annapurna Base Camp (4,130m) puts you inside a ring of 7,000 and 8,000m peaks, and many first-time trekkers do it. The [short version](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/annapurna-base-camp-short-trek) takes nine days; the [full trek](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/annapurna-base-camp-trek) takes 13 and is easier on the legs. Expect thousands of stone steps up and down around Chhomrong, and cold nights at the top. In winter, read our guide to [ABC in December and winter](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/annapurna-base-camp-trek-in-winter) first.

## Can a beginner do Everest Base Camp?

Yes, if you are fit and patient, but it is not an easy first trek. [Everest Base Camp](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/everest-base-camp-trek) takes about two weeks, you sleep above 4,900m for several nights, and Kala Patthar reaches 5,545m. Beginners who succeed usually train for two or three months, take every acclimatisation day and walk slowly. If you are unsure, start with the Everest View trek. Our [EBC cost guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/everest-base-camp-trek-cost) shows what the full trek involves.

## How to choose your first trek

- **Short on time:** Poon Hill or Everest View, both about a week.
- **Want to see Everest:** Everest View, or Everest Base Camp if you are fit.
- **Want to avoid flights:** Poon Hill, Khopra Danda, Mardi Himal or Langtang.
- **Want quiet trails:** Khopra Danda or Mardi Himal.
- **Want the biggest mountain scenery:** Annapurna Base Camp.

## How fit do you need to be?

You do not need trekking experience, but you should be able to walk uphill for 4 to 6 hours on several days in a row. Six to eight weeks of preparation is enough for most beginner treks: two or three hill walks a week, stairs with a light backpack, and practice walks in your boots. See our guide on [how to prepare for a Himalaya trek](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/how-to-prepare-for-a-himalaya-trek).

Altitude matters more than fitness above 3,000m. Walk slowly, drink plenty and read up on [preventing altitude sickness](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/how-to-prevent-altitude-sickness-while-trekking-in-nepal).

## Best time for a first trek

October to November and March to April give the clearest skies and the most comfortable temperatures. December to February are quiet and clear but cold; the lower routes, such as Poon Hill, work best then. See the [best treks in Nepal in December](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/best-treks-in-nepal-in-december). Avoid the June to September monsoon for a first trek.

## Do beginners need a guide?

Most first-time trekkers are better off with one, and on most routes it is required: since 2023, Nepal has required licensed guides in its national parks and conservation areas, although enforcement varies by region. Read [can you trek in Nepal without a guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/trekking-in-nepal-without-a-guide) for the details.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest trek in Nepal?

Poon Hill is usually considered the easiest multi-day trek: 3,210m at the highest point, 4 to 6 hours a day and lodges in every village.

### What is the best short trek in Nepal for beginners?

Poon Hill (3 to 5 days on the trail) or the Everest View trek (7 days including flights).

### What is the easiest base camp trek in Nepal?

Annapurna Base Camp is easier than Everest Base Camp: it is lower (4,130m against 5,364m) and shorter. Mardi Himal Base Camp (4,500m) is shorter still, but has one steep day.

### Can I trek in Nepal with no experience?

Yes. Choose a lower route such as Poon Hill, prepare for six to eight weeks and trek with a guide.

### Which beginner trek has the best mountain views?

Annapurna Base Camp for being surrounded by peaks, Mardi Himal for close-up views of Machhapuchhre, and Everest View for seeing Everest itself.
