# Mardi Himal Trek Cost 2026/27: Permits, Guide & Daily Budget

**Quick answer:** a guided Mardi Himal trek costs about **USD 600–1,000 per person** for 5–7 days, including
transport from Kathmandu, permits, a licensed guide, a porter, all meals and teahouse rooms. Trekking with only a
guide and paying as you go costs around **USD 400–500** for the same days. Permits alone are NPR 4,000 (about USD 30).
The biggest variables are how you travel Kathmandu–Pokhara and whether you hire a porter.

## Mardi Himal trek cost at a glance

| Way of trekking | Days on the trail | Typical cost per person | What it covers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full package with a Nepali agency (our 7-day trip) | 4 walking + 3 travel | USD 900–1,000 | Everything below except tips, drinks and insurance |
| Guide only, pay as you go | 4–5 | USD 400–500 | Guide, permits, your own rooms, meals and transport |
| Guide + porter, pay as you go | 4–5 | USD 500–600 | As above, plus a porter |
| Booked through an international tour site | 7–12 | USD 950–1,600 | Same trek, often resold with a margin |

Since April 2023 foreign trekkers in the Annapurna Conservation Area must trek with a licensed guide, so a true
"solo" budget no longer exists; the cheapest legal option is the guide-only line.

## 1. Permits: NPR 4,000 (about USD 30)

| Permit | Foreign nationals | SAARC nationals | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annapurna Conservation Area Permit (ACAP) | NPR 3,000 | NPR 1,000 | Nepal Tourism Board office in Pokhara or Kathmandu; double fee if issued at a checkpost |
| TIMS card | NPR 1,000 through an agency (NPR 2,000 independent) | NPR 600 / 1,000 | Same offices |

Children under 10 pay no ACAP fee. Carry two passport photos and a passport copy; your agency usually arranges both
permits the day before you leave Pokhara. Current fees are kept up to date on our
[trekking permits page](https://natureheaventreks.com/travel-info/trekking-permits-and-fees).

## 2. Guide and porter: USD 25–35 and 20–25 per day

- **Licensed trekking guide:** USD 25–35 per day, which covers the guide's wages, insurance, food and lodging on the
  trail. For a 5-day trek that is about USD 125–175.
- **Porter:** USD 20–25 per day, carrying up to about 18–20 kg (two trekkers' bags). About USD 100–125 for the trek.
- **Guide-porter** (one person who guides and carries a lighter load): USD 25–35 per day, a common compromise for
  couples on a budget.
- **Tips** are expected but not fixed: around USD 8–12 per day for the guide and USD 5–8 for the porter, from the
  group, given at the end.

Hiring through a registered agency costs a little more than finding someone in Pokhara, but the guide is insured,
licensed and replaced if ill, which matters on the ridge above High Camp.

## 3. Teahouses and food: NPR 2,500–4,000 per day

| | Lower trail (Kande to Forest Camp) | Higher (Low Camp, High Camp) |
|---|---|---|
| Twin room per night | NPR 500–1,000 | NPR 1,000–1,500 |
| Dal bhat (refilled) | NPR 550–700 | NPR 800–1,000 |
| Breakfast | NPR 400–600 | NPR 600–800 |
| Tea or coffee | NPR 100–200 | NPR 200–300 |
| Boiled drinking water, 1 litre | NPR 100–150 | NPR 200–300 |
| Hot shower | NPR 300–500 | often not available |
| Phone charging | free to NPR 200 | NPR 200–500 |
| Wi-Fi | free to NPR 300 | NPR 300–600 or none |

Plan on **NPR 2,500–4,000 (USD 18–30) a day** for a room and three meals if you pay as you go. Prices rise with
altitude because everything above Low Camp is carried up by mule or porter. In the October peak, High Camp lodges
sometimes ask more for a room and are often full by mid-afternoon, which is one reason a package with pre-booked
rooms is worth it in season.

## 4. Transport: USD 20–280 return

| Leg | Option | Cost per person |
|---|---|---|
| Kathmandu – Pokhara | Tourist bus, 6–8 hours | USD 10–25 each way |
| Kathmandu – Pokhara | Flight, 25 minutes | USD 90–130 each way |
| Pokhara – Kande (trailhead) | Private taxi or jeep, 1.5 hours, shared by the group | NPR 2,500–4,000 per vehicle |
| Siding – Pokhara (trail end) | Shared or private jeep, 2 hours | NPR 1,500–2,500 per person, or NPR 8,000–12,000 per private jeep |

The bus versus flight choice is the single biggest swing in the whole budget: about USD 20 return by bus against up
to USD 260 return by air.

## 5. What you pay for on the trail that packages never include

- **Travel insurance with helicopter evacuation:** USD 60–150 for a two-week policy, depending on your country and
  age. Not optional; a rescue from High Camp costs USD 3,000 or more. See our guide to
  [travel insurance for trekking in Nepal](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/travel-insurance-for-trekking-in-nepal).
- **Nepal visa on arrival:** USD 30 for 15 days, USD 50 for 30 days.
- **Gear:** renting a down jacket and a −10°C sleeping bag in Pokhara costs about NPR 150–250 per item per day.
- **Snacks, drinks, extra charging, Wi-Fi:** budget NPR 500–1,000 a day if you want them.

## Sample budget: 5 days on the trail, pay as you go with a guide

| Item | USD |
|---|---|
| Permits (ACAP + TIMS) | 30 |
| Guide, 5 days | 150 |
| Rooms and meals, 5 days at USD 25 | 125 |
| Tourist bus Kathmandu–Pokhara return | 25 |
| Taxi to Kande and jeep from Siding (share of) | 30 |

Add a porter and the total is about USD 520. Fly instead of the bus and it is about USD 650. Add two hotel nights in
Pokhara and one in Kathmandu and you arrive at the price of a package.

## How to trek Mardi Himal cheaper

1. **Take the bus to Pokhara.** Saves up to USD 240 per person compared with flying.
2. **Share a guide and porter.** One guide covers up to about eight people; the daily rate stays the same.
3. **Eat dal bhat.** It is the cheapest filling meal and the only one with free refills.
4. **Go in late November or March.** Rooms are easier to find and lodges are more flexible on prices than in October.
5. **Carry your own daypack and rent the big items in Pokhara** instead of buying a sleeping bag and down jacket.
6. **Avoid the 4-day itinerary to save money.** It looks cheaper but skips acclimatisation; turning back sick costs
   more than an extra night. The [difficulty guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/mardi-himal-trek-difficulty)
   explains why.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does the Mardi Himal trek cost in total?

About USD 600–1,000 per person for a guided package of 5–7 days from Kathmandu, or USD 400–500 trekking with a guide
and paying for rooms, meals and transport yourself.

### How much are the Mardi Himal permits?

NPR 4,000 for foreign nationals: the ACAP permit (NPR 3,000) and the TIMS card (NPR 1,000 through an agency, NPR 2,000
if you apply independently). Children under 10 are exempt from ACAP.

### How much does a guide cost for Mardi Himal?

USD 25–35 per day for a licensed guide and USD 20–25 per day for a porter, including their food, lodging and
insurance. A guide is required for foreign trekkers in the Annapurna Conservation Area.

### Can I do the Mardi Himal trek on a budget?

Yes. Bus to Pokhara, a shared guide, dal bhat and a rented sleeping bag bring a 5-day trek to roughly USD 400 per
person. The cost that should not be cut is insurance with helicopter cover.

### Is Mardi Himal cheaper than Annapurna Base Camp?

Yes, mainly because it is shorter: 4–5 trail days against 7–10. The daily costs are almost the same; permits are
identical.

### Does the price include the Pokhara hotel?

In our package, yes: two nights in Pokhara and one in Kathmandu with breakfast. Pay-as-you-go trekkers should add
USD 15–40 per night in Pokhara.
