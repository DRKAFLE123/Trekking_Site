# Poon Hill vs Mardi Himal: Which Short Trek Should You Choose?

**Quick answer:** Choose **Poon Hill** if it is your first trek, you are travelling with children, or you are trekking in mid-winter: it tops out at 3,210m and you sleep below 3,000m every night. Choose **Mardi Himal** if you are reasonably fit and want quieter trails and much closer mountain views: it climbs a ridge to 4,500m, right below Machhapuchhre. Both start from Pokhara and take about a week from Kathmandu.

## Poon Hill: the easier trek

[Poon Hill](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/poon-hill-trek) is the classic first trek in Nepal. You walk up stone staircases and through rhododendron forest to Ghorepani, climb Poon Hill before dawn for a wide view of Dhaulagiri, Annapurna South and Machhapuchhre, then descend through Tadapani and the Gurung village of Ghandruk. Days are 4 to 6 hours, there is a lodge or tea stop every hour, and the altitude rarely causes problems.

The downside is company: in October, November and April the viewpoint and lodges are busy. Our [Poon Hill itinerary guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/poon-hill-trek-for-beginners) has 3, 4 and 5-day plans with distances.

## Mardi Himal: the higher, quieter trek

[Mardi Himal](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/mardi-himal-trek) leaves the Poon Hill trails near Pitam Deurali and climbs through forest to Low Camp (2,970m) and High Camp (3,580m), then follows a narrow grassy ridge to Mardi Himal Base Camp (4,500m). Machhapuchhre (Fishtail) fills the view for most of the trek, and the trails are much quieter than Poon Hill's.

It is harder in two ways. You gain more than 1,000m between Forest Camp and High Camp in one day, which is where most people first feel the altitude, and the Base Camp day is 7 to 8 hours of steep walking. Read our [Mardi Himal difficulty guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/mardi-himal-trek-difficulty) and [Mardi Himal cost guide](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/mardi-himal-trek-cost) for the details.

## Difficulty compared

Poon Hill is the easier trek. Its hardest day, the climb from Tikhedhunga to Ghorepani, is tiring because of the steps, not the altitude. Mardi Himal's hardest day is longer and higher, and above High Camp the ridge can be windy and, in winter, icy. Neither trek has technical sections, and both are possible for fit beginners. If you have never trekked before and are unsure of your fitness, start with Poon Hill.

## Views compared

Poon Hill gives you one famous sunrise: a wide panorama of the Dhaulagiri and Annapurna ranges from a single hilltop. Mardi Himal gives you mountain views for most of three days, and they get closer every hour; from the ridge, Machhapuchhre and Annapurna South feel almost within reach. Photographers who want big, close faces of rock and ice usually prefer Mardi Himal.

## Which is better in winter?

Poon Hill. It stays low enough to be reliable from December to February, though the dawn hike is below freezing and stone steps can be icy early. Mardi Himal is excellent in early December, but nights at High Camp drop to about −8 to −12°C, and later in the month and in January snow can block the ridge, so you may have to turn back at the Upper Viewpoint. See [best treks in Nepal in December](https://natureheaventreks.com/blogs/best-treks-in-nepal-in-december).

## Can you combine Poon Hill and Mardi Himal?

Yes. The two trails meet near Pitam Deurali and around Ghandruk and Landruk, so you can join them in about 9 to 11 days on the trail, usually Poon Hill first and Mardi Himal second, so that you are acclimatised for the ridge. If you want a quieter alternative to Poon Hill with similar views, the [Khopra Danda trek](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/khopra-dada-trek) climbs to about 3,660m, and we also run [Khopra Danda with Mardi Himal](https://natureheaventreks.com/trips/khopra-danda-with-mardi-himal-trek).

## Choose Poon Hill if…

- It is your first trek, or you are trekking with children
- You want the lowest altitude and the most comfortable lodges
- You are trekking in mid-winter
- You have only 3 or 4 days on the trail

## Choose Mardi Himal if…

- You are fit and have done some hill walking
- You want quiet trails and close mountain views
- You would rather spend several days among the peaks than see one sunrise
- You are trekking in autumn, spring or early December

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Mardi Himal harder than Poon Hill?

Yes. Mardi Himal reaches 4,500m against Poon Hill's 3,210m, sleeps higher (3,580m at High Camp) and has one long, steep day to Base Camp.

### Which has better views, Poon Hill or Mardi Himal?

Poon Hill has the famous sunrise panorama; Mardi Himal has closer views for longer, especially of Machhapuchhre.

### Can beginners do Mardi Himal?

Fit beginners can, with a 6 or 7-day itinerary and a guide. If you are unsure, start with Poon Hill.

### How many days do you need for Poon Hill and Mardi Himal together?

About 9 to 11 days on the trail, plus travel to and from Pokhara.

### Which trek is less crowded?

Mardi Himal. Poon Hill is one of Nepal's busiest short treks in October, November and April.
